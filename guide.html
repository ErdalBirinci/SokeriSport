Field guide

In the gym, in ten minutes.

The simulator gives you a plan. This page is the routine around it: what to check, when to check it, and what to do when reality does not match the projection. It is deliberately short — you are standing in a gym, not reading a paper.

Before you start

  1. Glucose and arrowTake a fresh CGM reading plus a fingerstick if the arrow is moving. The plan is only as good as the number you feed it.
  2. IOBRead it from the pump or the bolus app. If you cannot, use the cautious mode and treat the result as a range, not a number.
  3. Time to the startFifteen to thirty minutes is the useful window: enough time for the carbohydrate to land, short enough that the trend still means something.
  4. Run the planCheck the verdict before the carbohydrate, not after. If it says stop, the session starts later.

Keep 15 g of fast carbohydrate in the pocket or the bottle cage. It is the one item that cannot be improvised at minute 60.

The first ten minutes

  • Easy paceStart below the planned intensity for two to three minutes. A hard first minute is how a plan gets abandoned.
  • Arrow checkIf the arrow has turned down while you were warming up, the in-session dose moves earlier rather than later.
  • One numberConfirm the start glucose within the first ten minutes; that value is what you compare against at the end.

During the session

SessionCheck everyFuel
Under 45 min20–30 minUsually none; water
45–90 min20–30 minThe hourly target from the plan, in 20–30 g doses
Over 90 min20 minSame hourly target, started earlier; electrolytes when it is warm
High intensity intervals15–20 minSmaller, more frequent doses; expect a rise in the first 20 min

A dose taken late is a dose that arrives after the drop. If two checks in a row are falling, take the next dose early and reduce the intensity, not the carbohydrate.

After the finish

  • Recovery carbohydrateWithin 30–60 minutes, with protein. This is the window where the muscle refills and the late drop is softened.
  • Log the outcomeTwo minutes in the simulator: start glucose, lowest reading, carbohydrate actually taken, whether a hypo happened. That entry is what makes the next plan better.
  • Late dropInsulin sensitivity stays raised for hours. Set a CGM alarm for the night after a long or hard session.
  • Export if neededBefore a clinic visit, export the CSV or print the plan; both carry the plan and the logged outcome side by side.

When the plan does not match reality

What you seeWhat it usually meansWhat to do next time
Falling much faster than projected IOB was higher, or the session was harder than the intensity you picked Log the outcome, then re-run the plan with the real IOB and one step higher intensity
Rising instead of falling Interval or strength work, or too much pre-session carbohydrate Reduce the pre-dose, keep the in-session fuel, start easier
Nadir later than projected Slow carbohydrate, or a long session Switch the carb type to fast, and move the first in-session dose earlier
Hypo despite the plan The plan was followed but the body did not cooperate Log it with the hypo flag; review the pattern with your care team rather than adjusting alone

One session is an anecdote. Three logged sessions of the same sport are a pattern worth acting on — with your team, not instead of them.

Low right now

Treat the low first.

  1. Stop. Sit down and end the session here.
  2. 15–20 g fast carbohydrate — 4 glucose tablets, 150 ml juice, or 1 tablespoon of sugar or honey.
  3. Wait 15 minutes. Do not take insulin and do not restart training.
  4. Recheck. Still below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L)? Repeat the dose.
  5. Once above 90 mg/dL (5.0), have a snack with slow carbohydrate; no exercise for 45 minutes.

If you cannot swallow, or the person is confused or unresponsive, follow emergency services — this card does not apply.
