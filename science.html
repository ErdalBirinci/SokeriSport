Rule engine, formulas and sources

Every output from SokeriSport is derived from the thresholds and formulas below. The engine is a pure function: the same set of inputs always produces the same plan. There is no randomness, no learned model and no server-side personalisation.

Calculation flow

  1. Normalisationmmol/L → mg/dL, range clamping, defaults for missing fields.
  2. ProjectionPer-minute rate of the trend arrow × time remaining until exercise.
  3. IOB loadIOB × sensitivity factor × sport multiplier; limited by a safe floor.
  4. Exercise drawOxidation rate × duration × carb/glucose coefficient × muscle share.
  5. Carb doseNet gap ÷ carb coefficient; body-weight cap and trend correction.
  6. Basal scenarioCategory baseline + duration/IOB/glucose adjustments, clamped to 0–80%.
  7. SimulationPlanned and unplanned glucose curves in 5-minute steps, risk scores, warnings.

Safety thresholds

These thresholds are applied in order during the pre-exercise assessment; the first matching rule decides the verdict.

ConditionDecisionRationale
Glucose < 70 mg/dL No exercise Hypoglycemia treatment comes first: 15–20 g fast carbs, recheck after 15 min.
Ketones ≥ moderate and glucose ≥ 250 mg/dL No exercise Risk of ketoacidosis; fluids and ketone monitoring, involve your care team if needed.
Glucose ≥ 300 mg/dL Postpone High-intensity exercise is not advised without checking ketones.
Estimated start < 90 mg/dL Take carbs Target band is 90–180 mg/dL; starting below it accelerates the fall.
Simulated nadir < 70 mg/dL Plan / postpone IOB and muscle draw together produce hypoglycemia.
Simulated nadir < 85 mg/dL or IOB > 2 U Caution Check every 20–30 min, carry backup carbs.
Otherwise Safe Plan is workable; first check recommended within 30 min.

Formulas

1 · Projection Gstart = Gnow + (rtrend × tleft)

r is the per-minute rate of the trend arrow (⇊ −3 … ⇈ +3), t is minutes left until exercise.

2 · IOB load DIOB = min( IOB × ISF × ksport , max(0, Gstart − 85) )

ksport is the sport's insulin sensitivity multiplier. The upper bound excludes the part that cannot be safely covered with carbohydrate — it does not hide the "no amount of carbs will fix this" case, it turns it into a postpone recommendation.

3 · Muscle draw Dmuscle = Ohour × thour × Kcho × 0.35

O is hourly carbohydrate oxidation (g/h), Kcho is the effect of 1 gram of carbohydrate on plasma glucose (≈3.5 mg/dL at 70 kg). 0.35 is the muscle glycogen share.

4 · Carb dose g = ( H − Gstart + DIOB + Dmuscle − A ) / Kcho

H is the target glucose, A is the catecholamine credit (the temporary rise in the first 20 min of high-intensity sports). The result is rounded to 5 g and capped by min(50 g, 0.6 × kg).

5 · Basal reduction % = clamp( base + Δduration + ΔIOB + Δglucose , 0 , 80 )

Base: high-intensity 50%, endurance 40%, mixed 35%, strength 20%, light 25%. Duration >2.5 h +25, 1.5–2.5 h +15; IOB ≥1.5 U −10; start ≤100 mg/dL +10.

6 · Recovery CHOafter = kg × (0.3 – 1.2)

Between 0.3 g/kg (short) and 1.2 g/kg (2 h+, high intensity) depending on duration and intensity. Sessions over 1 hour add 0.3 g/kg protein (max. 30 g).

Simulation model

The projection advances in 5-minute steps as the sum of four components:

Insulin IOB is spread evenly over its duration of action; it is amplified by the sensitivity multiplier during exercise.
Trend The current trend decays to zero with a 30-minute time constant — a temporary event is prevented from lasting forever.
Muscle draw Constant glucose draw during the session; an adrenaline rise in the first 20 min for high-intensity sports.
Carbohydrate Pre-load: 10 min delay + 35 min absorption (slow carbohydrate: +15 min, ×1.9 duration, ×0.75 plasma share); in-session doses: 5 min delay + 30 min absorption.
Counter-regulation Below 90 mg/dL a partial physiological defence is added; hypoglycemia is not hidden, but stays realistic.

The model is clamped to 40–350 mg/dL. Two curves are then produced, planned and unplanned; the difference between them shows what the app contributes.

Limitations

  • Individual insulin sensitivity varies through the day and with stress, sleep and the menstrual cycle; the engine assumes a constant ISF.
  • IOB is approximated with a single-compartment, linear distribution. The real curve depends on the insulin analogue.
  • Meal carbohydrate, fat/protein content, temperature, altitude and alcohol are not modelled.
  • The simulation covers a 3-hour window; the probability of overnight late hypoglycemia is reported separately as a risk score.
  • Results should be calibrated with your own data: in the first weeks, compare the engine's estimate with your actual CGM trace.
Clinical notice. This app does not diagnose or adjust treatment. Decide on insulin doses, basal changes and carbohydrate amounts together with your diabetes team. If you feel hypoglycemia symptoms (shaking, sweating, confusion), stop exercising and take fast carbohydrate.
